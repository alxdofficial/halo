# Phase-B end-to-end episodic training: four arms, and why none of them works

> **SUPERSEDED 2026-08-19 by [`PHASE_B_MIXER_20260819.md`](PHASE_B_MIXER_20260819.md).**
> The four arms below genuinely do not work, and that finding stands. The *title's* wider claim does
> not: the evidence mixer added on 2026-08-19 is worth **+0.119** on this same protocol against the
> same control, with k=0 going 0.3916 -> 0.5351. Read this file as the record of what failed and
> why, not as the state of the model.

**Date** 2026-08-18 · **Encoder** `phase_a_h_mae_fixes_20260818/best.pt` · **Trainer**
`training/tokenizer/pretrain_episodic.py` · **Protocol** held-out concepts, held-out subjects,
shared-query-stream episodes with a measured acquisition-provenance lift of **0.0 points**.

All numbers are hard top-k (the deployment rule, `top_k=64`), macro-F1 in label space, meaned over
the fixed support grid k = 0, 1, 2, 4. 96 validation episodes per checkpoint, 13 checkpoints per arm.

## 1. The four arms

| arm | what trains | hard mean | k=0 | k=4 | transfer | val std |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **control** | nothing | **0.4208** | 0.3876 | 0.4435 | 0.8471 | — |
| gate only | 6,209 gate params | 0.4027 | 0.3795 | 0.4230 | 0.8471 | 0.0047 |
| e2e, anchor 0.5 | encoder + gate, drift priced | 0.4160 | 0.3875 | 0.4324 | **0.8534** | 0.0041 |
| e2e, anchor 0 | encoder + gate | **0.4390** | 0.3896 | 0.4753 | **0.7928** | 0.0130 |

Reference points for `transfer` (`development_transfer_score`, subject-disjoint kNN on development
streams in no training corpus): random-init floor **0.8012**, this checkpoint **0.8468**, best ever
**0.8577**.

### What each arm establishes

**Control.** All gradients exactly 0.0, validation byte-identical across all 13 checkpoints. The
floor is real and the loop is honest.

**Gate only — training the gate ALONE makes things worse.** 0.4208 → 0.4027, best value is step 0,
monotone decline, `gate/mean` 0.501 → 0.290. This independently reproduces what the shipping table
already says: *"adaptation exists, but learned admissibility has no held-out advantage."* It also
matches the Stage-2 record, where the warm-started gate scores 0.3837 against 0.6003 for no gate at
all, and 1,000 refinement steps mostly repair its own damage.

**anchor 0.5 — the gain and the damage are the same thing.** Pricing drift preserves the
representation completely (transfer 0.8534, *above* where it started) and removes the entire task
gain (0.4160, no better than frozen). The encoder was not learning anything transferable about
retrieval; it was spending general representation quality on episode-specific fit. Validation
variance also collapses 0.0130 → 0.0041, so most of the unanchored arm's movement was drift, not
learning.

**anchor 0 — fails its pre-registered gate.** `transfer` falls monotonically
0.8471 → 0.8184 → 0.7987 → 0.7919 → 0.7928, crossing **below the random-init floor** between steps
1,500 and 2,250: the encoder ends worse on held-out transfer than an *untrained* encoder of the same
architecture. The +0.018 task gain sits inside the arm's own noise (std 0.0130, dips to 0.3978 —
below control — at step 2,250), and "best 0.4406" is a max over 13 checkpoints, i.e. a selection
artifact.

## 2. Why: only 18.8% of the encoder is trainable, and it is the wrong 18.8%

`python -m training.tokenizer.diagnose_episodic` measured which parameters receive gradient:

```
815,872 / 4,337,792 = 18.8%
```

Live: `filterbank.proj`, `sensor_fold`, `layers.0.temporal_attn` + `norm1`.
Dead: `descriptor_proj` (296k), `layers.1` and `layers.2` (2.1M), and inside layer 0 the
`feed_forward`, `cross_channel_attn`, `norm2`, `norm3` (790k).

This is the design of record made visible, not a bug: Phase B retrieves on `retrieval_tokens`,
emitted *before* the trunk and before descriptor conditioning. So task pressure lands only on the
earliest, most general stage — the inverse of ordinary fine-tuning, which adapts late layers and
protects early ones. Erosion of a general metric is the predicted outcome, and it is what happened.

## 3. The vote imbalance is real — but rebalancing it does not help

`retrieval/enrolled_vote_mass_share` sits at **2.5–6%** across full-scale episodes: enrolled
evidence carries almost none of the decision. Measured anatomy at k=4, 8 candidates:

| quantity | value |
|---|---|
| bound (support) rows per episode | 167.7 |
| unbound (corpus) rows per episode | 561.8 (1 : 3.4) |
| retrieval mass on bound rows | 38.3% |
| retrieval mass on corpus rows | 61.7% |
| vote per bound row | 1.000, to **one** candidate |
| vote per corpus row | 0.270, to **every** candidate (2.16 summed over 8) |
| label-text cosine between distinct labels | mean **0.338**, min 0.112, max 0.824 |

That last row is the pedestal: every corpus row votes ~0.34 for essentially every candidate
regardless of relevance.

**Three rebalancings were tested with no training, on fixed episodes, 200 episodes, held-out
concepts. Variant A reproduces the shipped `vote()` to 3.05e-05, so the comparison is valid.**

| variant | k=0 | k=1 | k=2 | k=4 | mean | vs current |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A — current (additive)** | 0.1637 | 0.2585 | 0.3421 | 0.3686 | **0.2832** | — |
| B — centred text vote `relu((cos−c0)/(1−c0))` | 0.1601 | 0.2348 | 0.3211 | 0.3650 | 0.2702 | −0.0130 |
| C — channel-balanced (mean per channel) | 0.1568 | 0.2420 | 0.3240 | 0.2215 | 0.2361 | −0.0471 |
| D — centred + balanced | 0.1587 | 0.2385 | 0.3154 | 0.2365 | 0.2373 | −0.0459 |
| E — enrolled votes only | 0.1371 | 0.2258 | 0.3017 | 0.3380 | 0.2507 | −0.0326 |
| F — corpus text votes only | 0.1637 | 0.2378 | 0.3164 | 0.3511 | 0.2673 | −0.0160 |

**Every variant is worse.** The current additive rule beats centring, beats channel balancing, and
beats either channel alone. Channel balancing is worst precisely where it was supposed to help — k=4
collapses 0.3686 → 0.2215 — because enrolled evidence is retrieved only 17–22% of the time, so
up-weighting that channel amplifies noise rather than signal.

**The hypothesis that motivated this experiment is therefore rejected.** "Enrollment carries 3% of
the mass" is a correct *description*; "rebalance the channels" is not a fix. The 3% appears to be
roughly the weight that noisy channel deserves.

## 4. What the depth probe says (and a correction)

Ridge probe on this checkpoint, subject-disjoint, three development cohorts:

| representation | activity | subject | margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| `retrieval_row` (deployed) | 0.8617 | 0.5688 | +0.2929 |
| depth 1 | 0.8738 | 0.5739 | +0.2998 |
| depth 2 | **0.8797** | 0.5610 | +0.3187 |
| depth 3 / pooled | 0.8698 | 0.5387 | **+0.3311** |

**Every trunk depth beats the deployed layer-0 row**, and the selectivity margin rises monotonically
with depth while subject leakage falls. The earlier finding that "the trunk destroys activity signal,
−4 to −8 points by depth 6" was measured on a **six-layer** trunk and is the reason it was cut to
three; at three layers the effect has reversed. Any claim that the retrieval row must stay shallow
*for representation-quality reasons* is stale.

What survives is the architectural reason in `encoder.py:476`: the row is taken before cross-sensor
mixing so an accelerometer row does not depend on whether a gyroscope coexisted. Depth 1–3 rows are
post-mixing and post-descriptor-conditioning, so they entangle acquisition configuration — and the
provenance probe reads dataset identity from the pooled vector at BA 0.702. This probe measures
activity and subject, **not configuration**, so it does not price that cost.

## 5. Where this leaves the design

Every trainable component in Phase B is now measured, and each is neutral or negative:

| component | params | measured effect |
|---|---|---|
| admissibility gate | 6,209 | −0.018 alone; historically −0.217 warm-started, +0.020 after Stage 2 vs no gate |
| encoder retrieval path | 815,872 | +0.018 only while eroding transfer by 0.054; 0.000 when drift is priced |
| vote recombination | 0 | all three tested variants worse than the closed form |

Meanwhile the frozen representation plus a plain ridge head beats UniMTS by **+7.31** at k=4. The
representation is not the bottleneck, and the mechanism on top of it is already near what training
can reach.

### The three experiments worth running next, in order

1. **Richer label descriptions** (task #148, never run). The 0.338 common mode is the largest single
   pathology measured here, and centring — a *linear* correction — failed. Discriminative
   descriptions attack it non-linearly, add no parameters, and change no architecture.
2. **Deeper retrieval row, kept sensor-isolated.** Include layer-0's FFN (→1.34M trainable) or stack
   temporal-only blocks (→2.13M) — captures the measured depth benefit without cross-sensor mixing.
   Needs a configuration-identity probe per depth first, which does not yet exist.
3. **A config-leakage probe by depth**, which is the missing measurement blocking (2).

### The framing question this raises

The evidence increasingly supports describing Phase B as *a principled closed-form retrieval
mechanism over a configuration-conditional representation*, rather than a learned adapter. That is a
defensible contribution and it is what the numbers actually show. Continuing to present learned
admissibility as the contribution is not supported by any held-out measurement taken so far.
